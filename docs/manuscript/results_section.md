## Section: Executed Measurement Study

### A. Setup Recap

We executed the measurement study that Section V specifies, on an
open, deterministic mock rig released with this paper. The seven
patterns of Section IV are instantiated twice each, against locally
mocked Salesforce Agentforce 360 and Amazon Bedrock/AgentCore platform
clients that reproduce the *interface shapes* of the real platforms
(`invoke_agent`, AgentCore gateway/memory/identity/observability,
topics→actions, Agent Script, platform events, Omni-Channel handoff);
service times are drawn from per-platform, per-service lognormal
models and all timing is virtual, so the study is exactly reproducible
from a fixed seed. The baseline grid crosses 7 patterns × 2 platforms
× 3 scenarios = 42 conditions with n = 500 requests per condition
(trace: results/manifest.json fields n_baseline_conditions,
n_per_condition), generated by an open-loop Poisson load generator at
2.0 requests/s against a concurrency-4 server pool (trace:
configs/default.yaml load.rate_rps, load.concurrency); submit and
completion timestamps are recorded separately, so reported latencies
include queueing and are free of coordinated omission. The fault
campaign sweeps 336 cells = 7 patterns × 2 platforms × 6 components ×
4 fault types at 40 requests per cell on scenario S1 (trace:
results/manifest.json fields fault_campaign_cells,
fault_campaign_n_per_cell, fault_campaign_scenario). The master seed
is 42 and the configuration hash, git revision, and wall time
(2.663 s) are recorded in the run manifest (trace:
results/manifest.json fields seed, config_hash, git_rev, wall_time_s).
All percentile estimates carry 95% BCa bootstrap confidence intervals;
pairwise pattern comparisons use Mann–Whitney U with rank-biserial and
Hodges–Lehmann effect estimates under Holm correction over the 21-pair
family per condition, per the pre-committed plan
(docs/STATISTICAL_ANALYSIS_PLAN.md).

### B. Latency Results

Table 3 (figures/table3.csv) reports p50/p95/p99 with CIs for all 42
conditions; Fig. ccdf.png and Fig. p99_ci.png visualize the
distributions. Three structural effects dominate.

First, event-driven choreography (P3) is the fastest topology on the
CRM platform: on Agentforce under S1 it achieves a p50 of 129.1 ms
(95% CI 125.5–131.1) and a p99 of 214.1 ms (95% CI 199.6–229.3)
(trace: figures/table3.csv row P3,agentforce,S1 cols p50_ms,
p50_ci_lo, p50_ci_hi, p99_ms, p99_ci_lo, p99_ci_hi; values rounded to
0.1 ms), an order of magnitude below the supervisor fan-out P1 on the
same platform and scenario (p50 1179.4 ms, CI 1128.4–1218.5; p99
3189.8 ms, CI 2700.3–3729.3) (trace: figures/table3.csv row
P1,agentforce,S1).

Second, fan-out and shared-state topologies saturate the open-loop rig
at the studied rate, and the open-loop measurement makes the resulting
queueing visible rather than hiding it: P1 on Bedrock under S1 — five
model invocations per request (trace: figures/cost_ledger.csv row
P1,bedrock col mean_model_invocations) — exhibits a queueing-dominated
p50 of 61121.7 ms (95% CI 57348.1–65063.5) and p99 of 114396.6 ms
(95% CI 113816.7–115427.6) (trace: figures/table3.csv row
P1,bedrock,S1), and the blackboard P4 on Agentforce shows the same
regime (p50 43275.4 ms, p99 81365.1 ms; trace: figures/table3.csv row
P4,agentforce,S1). These are unsustainable operating points in the
Little's-law sense and are graded accordingly in Table 4; a
closed-loop harness would have under-reported them.

Third, the long-horizon scenario S2 penalizes deep pipelines most on
the platform whose per-stage cost is a full model invocation: P2 on
Bedrock under S2 records a p99 of 188916.5 ms (95% CI
187510.7–190554.0) versus 1666.2 ms (CI 1537.4–1713.5) for P2 on
Agentforce, whose Agent Script stages are lightweight actions (trace:
figures/table3.csv rows P2,bedrock,S2 and P2,agentforce,S2). The
human-oversight pattern P6 — measured here without human think-time,
i.e., the floor imposed by the pattern's machinery alone — remains
fast on both platforms (Bedrock S1: p50 533.5 ms, p99 1747.2 ms;
trace: figures/table3.csv row P6,bedrock,S1).

Across all 42 baseline conditions the measured error rate is 0.0
(trace: figures/table3.csv col error_rate, all rows), as expected with
fault injection disarmed; reliability differences appear only under
the fault campaign below.

### C. Cost Results

Fig. cost_per_1k.png and the per-pattern ledger
(figures/cost_ledger.csv) report cost in configured cost units under
the pricing assumptions of configs/costs.yaml (HUMAN-gated
assumptions; sources and dates in the YAML comments). The cost
hierarchy is structural, not incidental. On the mocked Agentforce
side, patterns that consume a platform model invocation per request
(P1, P5, P6, P7) cost ≈0.5 cost units per request (trace:
figures/cost_ledger.csv rows P1,agentforce; P5,agentforce;
P6,agentforce; P7,agentforce col mean_cost_units), the blackboard P4
costs 1.5000045 units per request with 3 model invocations and 6
service calls (trace: figures/cost_ledger.csv row P4,agentforce cols
mean_cost_units, mean_model_invocations, mean_service_calls), while
the action/event topologies P2 and P3 cost only 4.013e-06 and 3.0e-06
units respectively (trace: figures/cost_ledger.csv rows P2,agentforce
and P3,agentforce col mean_cost_units) because they invoke no model at
all under these scenarios. On the mocked Bedrock side, token-metered
pricing compresses the range: P1 averages 0.028078 units per request
across scenarios (5.0 model invocations; trace: figures/cost_ledger.csv
row P1,bedrock cols mean_cost_units, mean_model_invocations), the
pipeline P2 0.022554 (trace: figures/cost_ledger.csv row P2,bedrock
col mean_cost_units), and the single-invocation patterns P5/P6/P7
cluster near 0.0056–0.0063 units (trace: figures/cost_ledger.csv rows
P5,bedrock; P6,bedrock; P7,bedrock col mean_cost_units). Per-scenario
cost-per-request values appear in Table 3's cost_per_request column
(trace: figures/table3.csv col cost_per_request).

### D. Fault-Isolation Results

Fig. fault_matrix.png classifies each of the 336 campaign cells as
absorbed (fault traversed, requests still succeed), isolated (fault
traversed, only traversing requests fail; non-traversing success rate
≥ 0.95 per the containment contract), propagated (failures spread to
requests that never traversed the faulted component), or not exercised
(the pattern never touches the component). The executed campaign
yields 28 absorbed, 72 isolated, 0 propagated, and 236 not-exercised
cells (trace: results/faults.csv, cells classified by the rule in
agentorch/study/figures_fault.py: n_traversing == 0 → not exercised;
contained == False → propagated; traversing_success_rate ≥ 0.95 →
absorbed; else isolated). No cell propagated: in every exercised
cell the success rate of non-traversing requests remained 1.0 (trace:
results/faults.csv col non_traversing_success_rate, minimum over all
336 rows). Throttle faults are absorbed almost everywhere (25 of the
28 absorbed cells are throttle cells; trace: results/faults.csv,
absorbed cells grouped by col fault) because throttling delays but
does not fail a request; timeout, error, and outage faults produce the
72 isolated cells (24 each; trace: results/faults.csv, isolated cells
grouped by col fault). The gateway pattern P5 on Bedrock illustrates
the bulkhead effect the catalog predicts: a tool outage leaves
85.29% of traversing requests succeeding (only calls routed to the
faulted tool fail; trace: results/faults.csv row
P5,bedrock,S1,tool,outage col traversing_success_rate ≈ 0.852941)
while non-traversing requests are untouched (col
non_traversing_success_rate = 1.0). The bridge pattern P7 confines a
full model-backend outage to its own platform cluster (trace:
results/faults.csv rows P7,bedrock,S1,model_backend,* and
P7,agentforce,S1,model_backend,*, all contained == True).

### E. Threats to Validity

*Construct (mock fidelity).* The platforms are local mocks that
reproduce documented interface shapes and configured latency/cost
distributions, not the real Agentforce or Bedrock services. Absolute
latencies and costs therefore carry no claim about production
platforms; the study's claims are *comparative and structural* —
ordering and isolation properties that follow from coordination
topology under a common synthetic environment. The lognormal
parameters (configs/default.yaml latency section) and unit prices
(configs/costs.yaml) are assumptions, flagged as HUMAN-gated in the
repository; the full study reruns in 2.663 s wall time (trace:
results/manifest.json field wall_time_s) so sensitivity to these
assumptions is cheap to explore.

*Internal.* All randomness is seeded (seed 42; trace:
results/manifest.json field seed) and same-seed runs are bit-identical,
eliminating run-to-run variance as a confounder but meaning the
reported CIs quantify sampling variability within the run, not
environmental variability.

*External (synthetic workloads).* The three scenarios are synthetic
stress shapes (S1 single-step retrieval QA, S2 4–8 stage document
generation, S3 bursty triage with burst factor 5.0; trace:
configs/default.yaml scenarios section); real enterprise traffic mixes
will differ, and the rate/concurrency operating point (2.0 rps,
c = 4) was chosen to expose saturation behavior, not to model a
production deployment.

*Statistical.* Error rates of 0.0 in baseline mode make reliability
grades depend entirely on the fault campaign; with n = 40 per fault
cell, per-cell proportions are coarse. The Holm-corrected pairwise
family controls the latency comparisons of Table 4 but no correction
spans the full 42-condition grid.
